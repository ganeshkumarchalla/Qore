One connected workflow

From source code to inspectable evidence.

QORE supports bounded Qiskit circuit-building syntax and OpenQASM 2. It makes the relationship between your circuit, theoretical state, sampled measurements, and saved experiment explicit.

01 / STUDIO

A workspace you create.

Edit files and folders, inspect circuit gates, and start with an optional editable three-qubit GHZ circuit. New accounts receive no preloaded project files.

02 / QUANTUM LAB

See what the circuit does.

Explore measured counts, ideal probabilities, interactive Bloch spheres, state amplitudes, unitary matrices, and execution metadata. Compare finite-shot results with their mathematical reference.

03 / FORGE

Review a transformation.

Generate a separate compiler candidate, compare gate counts and depth, and inspect operator-equivalence evidence within the supported scope before approving any source change.

04 / LEDGER

Keep the whole experiment.

Save source, settings, seed, dependency versions, simulation, and optimization evidence as immutable snapshots. Reopen a result without changing its recorded history.

05 / ASSISTANT

Ask beside your work.

A dedicated secondary sidebar keeps optional AI explanations close to your active circuit and recorded results. Suggestions remain advisory and source changes stay under your control.

06 / YOUR ACCOUNT

A private place to explore.

Verified account sessions and owner-scoped cloud records separate your projects from other accounts. Local browser files stay in separate account caches.

Clear boundaries make results useful.

Simulation supports up to 10 qubits, 500 operations, and 100,000 shots. Full operator-equivalence checking is limited to six qubits. Displayed matrices and state readouts have their own size and precision limits.

QORE is a bounded circuit environment. It does not run arbitrary Python or provide a general system shell. Simulator noise is a model, and simulation output is not a quantum-hardware result or evidence of quantum advantage.
